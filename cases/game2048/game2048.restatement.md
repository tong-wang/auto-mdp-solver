## The problem

You are playing **2048** on an `n × n` grid. Each turn you slide the whole
board in one of four directions. Every line of tiles compresses toward the
wall it travels to, and **two adjacent equal tiles merge into one of double
the value** — resolved from that wall outward, and a tile formed by a merge
cannot merge again within the same slide (`[2,2,2]` slid left gives `[4,2]`).

If the slide moved or merged anything, **one new tile spawns into a uniformly
chosen empty cell** — a 2 most of the time, a 4 with probability `prob_4`. If
the slide would change nothing, it is a no-op: no merge, no score, no spawn.

The episode ends when the board is full and **no** slide is legal. Your score
is the game's own: **every merge that produces a tile of value `v` pays `v`**,
and the episode total is the final 2048 score.

What makes it hard is that the two things you want are in tension. Merging
frees cells and keeps you alive, but the tiles worth the most points are the
ones you have refused to spend. Meanwhile the spawn fills a cell you do not
choose, so every slide is taken against an adversary that is indifferent
rather than hostile — and the board is small enough that a few careless moves
leave no legal slide at all.

**There is no exact DP.** Tile values are unbounded, so the state space cannot
be enumerated. The strong reference is a depth-limited **expectimax** over the
true chance model, optionally with a snake-ordering leaf heuristic.

## Mode stance

Three axes are declared in the IR, and each is a *rendering* choice — the same
world seen through a different interface. None of them changes the problem.

| axis | modes | default | what differs |
|---|---|---|---|
| observation | `vec`, `grid`, `onehot` | `vec` | all three reference the **same** `board` variable; only the tensor the wrapper builds differs — flat log2 (MLP), `(1,n,n)` log2 (CNN), or `(K,n,n)` binary planes with plane `k` marking tiles of value `2^k` (CNN) |
| action | `masked`, `free` | `masked` | whether the agent is *prevented* from choosing an illegal slide. `valid_actions` lives in the mdp layer; under `free` an illegal slide is a no-op that still consumes a move |
| reward | `score`, `score_penalty` | `score` | `score` is the objective itself; `score_penalty` deducts `invalid_penalty` (4.0) per illegal slide. **Shaping only** — it is not in `objective.per_step_components`, so evaluation and every baseline score pure merge points regardless of how a policy was trained |

## Randomness classification

Two sources, on **separate streams**, both `keyed`:

| slot | stream | family | keyed on | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `spawn_cell` | 0 | `categorical` over `empty_cells(board)` | `spawn_count` | which empty cell the tile lands in |
| `spawn_value` | 1 | `bernoulli(prob_4)` | `spawn_count` | 4 or 2; dynamics map it with `2 + 2*four` |

Two properties follow, and both are load-bearing:

**The spawn position is state-dependent and therefore NOT decision-path
independent.** Its support is `empty_cells(board)`, resolved from the live
namespace at draw time — and which cells are free is exactly what the agent's
moves determine. Only the seed key is path-independent. `mdp_ir.laws`
classifies both slots **state-conditioned** and skips the path-independence
law rather than failing a correctly modelled domain.

**Both slots key on `spawn_count`, not `period`.** Spawns are counted in
board-*changing* slides, so a given episode seed produces the same tile
sequence no matter how many illegal slides the agent wastes. That is what
makes `masked` and `free` comparable on shared seeds.

## Decision, horizon, objective

- **Decision** — `move`, `discrete`, `dim` 1, bounds `[0, 3]`:
  0=Up, 1=Down, 2=Right, 3=Left. (`source: human_confirmed` — the game offers
  exactly four slides; there is nothing continuous to encode.)
- **State** — `period` (moves taken, including no-ops), `board`
  (`int_vector`, length `n_cells`, row-major; cell `(r,c)` is index
  `r*grid_size + c`, 0 = empty), `spawn_count`.
- **Horizon** — `T = T_cap`, 0-based. The game ends on its own at game over
  (`early_terminated_when`), so `horizon_end` is **`truncated`**: `T_cap` is an
  external move cap, not the problem. Under `masked` it is slack; under `free`
  it is the only thing that bounds a stalling policy.
- **Objective** — `maximize`, discount **β = 1.0** (it is a game), one
  per-step component: `merge = gained`, the game's own score for the move.

## Claims that must hold (declared as `mdp.invariants`)

| name | claim in words |
|---|---|
| `sum_conserved` | sliding only rearranges and combines tiles, so the board total can change only by the tile that spawns |
| `illegal_move_scores_nothing` | a slide that cannot move or merge anything earns no points |
| `illegal_move_spawns_nothing` | …and leaves the board exactly as it was. **Period 0 is exempt** — it also carries the opening spawn, which fires whether or not the first slide is legal (F3) |
| `game_over_needs_a_full_board` | the game can only be over when every cell is occupied |
| `score_cannot_exceed_the_board` | every point scored came from tiles already on the board |

These are the only checks the differential structurally **cannot** make: both
the interpreter and `game2048_mdp` call `game2048_board`, so the merge rule has
no independent implementation to be diffed against. A claim taken from the
rules of the game is the only thing that can falsify it — which is how F3 was
caught while the differential was MATCHing bit-for-bit.

## What the policy observes

Fully observed: **`requires_memory: false`** (`source: human_confirmed` — the
board is the entire state and the agent sees all of it; nothing is hidden to
infer). `frame_stack: 1`. Algorithm `maskable_ppo`.

**`obs_normalization` is deliberately off**: tile magnitudes grow all episode,
so VecNormalize's running statistics would chase a moving target instead of
standardising a stationary one. The wrapper's log2 scaling puts features on a
comparable range without it.

## Scenario set & training target

Scenario constants: `grid_size` / `n_cells` (axis `board`), `prob_4` (axis
`spawn`), `T_cap` (axis `sizing`), `invalid_penalty`.

Naming is `{n}x{n}_{prob_4 × 100}`. The base composition is **`3x3_20`**
(`grid_size` 3, `prob_4` 0.2, `T_cap` 2048); seven further instances are
declared:

| | `prob_4` = 0.0 | `prob_4` = 0.2 |
|---|---|---|
| 2×2 | `2x2_0` | `2x2_20` |
| 3×3 | `3x3_0` | **`3x3_20`** (base) |
| 4×4 | `4x4_0` | `4x4_20` |
| 5×5 | `5x5_0` | `5x5_20` |

`T_cap` is derived, not guessed: `max(200, 2^(n_cells+2))`. Masked play is
bounded by `2^(n_cells+1)` moves — every spawn adds 2 or 4 and slides conserve
the sum — so the cap is at least 2× the theoretical limit at every scale and
can never distort masked play.

**`grid_size` cannot be a generalist axis.** Board size changes the
observation shape, so one policy cannot span 3×3 and 4×4: board size is an
instance axis with its own leaderboard, and only `prob_4` is available for a
`GRIDS` entry — a weak axis, since 0 vs 0.2 barely changes optimal play.

## Key assumptions (with the `source` the IR records)

- **Opening tile** — the episode opens with **one** tile from the guarded `I`
  event at period 0, then a second spawns after the first slide if it was
  legal. The physical game opens with two; this follows the pre-IR domain. The
  IR cannot draw inside `initial_state`, so the opening spawn had to become a
  period-0 event either way (F2).
- **Move encoding** — 0=Up, 1=Down, 2=Right, 3=Left, carried over unchanged
  from the pre-IR domain.
- **Board is flat** — a length-`n_cells` row-major vector at the mdp layer;
  the 2-D shape is reconstructed in the gym, where it belongs.
- **`board.element_bounds` is a literal, not symbolic** — the exact ceiling is
  `2**n_cells`, but arithmetic on a constant is evaluated eagerly at load time
  and frozen for every instance (silently: `2x2_20` and `5x5_20` both reported
  the base 3×3 value). A generous literal (131072) is the honest choice; these
  bounds are metadata only, and the gym sizes its own spaces from `grid_size`.
- **Two spawn streams** — position and value draw separately rather than
  sharing one rng as the pre-IR generator did, so trajectories differ from the
  February 2026 runs.
- **Score definition** — a merge producing `v` pays `v`. This is **not** the
  pre-IR domain's `sum` reward, which accumulated the change in board total:
  since a slide conserves the sum, that telescopes to total spawned value and
  measures survival rather than play quality.

Four things in this trace are the model's distinctive behaviour, not noise:

- **`t=0` has `move_valid = 0` and yet `spawned = 2`.** The first slide was
  illegal on an empty board, but the opening `I` event fires anyway and
  `spawn_count` advances to 1. This is precisely why
  `illegal_move_spawns_nothing` carries its `t == 0` exemption (F3) — the
  first draft of that invariant failed on 7 of 20 seeds, all at `t=0`, while
  the differential MATCHed throughout.
- **`t=11`, `t=12`: `move_valid = 0`, `spawned = 0`, board byte-identical,
  and `spawn_count` frozen at 11.** The no-op is total, and because the spawn
  keys on `spawn_count` rather than `period`, two wasted moves cost the agent
  no tiles from its seeded sequence.
- **`t=10` pays 12 and `t=13` pays 20** — more than one merge can resolve in a
  single slide, and each pays the value of the tile it creates (8+4, then
  16+4).
- **`merge`, `total` and `reward` agree on every row.** Reward mode `score`
  *is* the objective; nothing is shaped here.
